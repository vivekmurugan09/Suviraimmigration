Visa Requirements

Canada Study Permit Eligibility & Financials

Understanding IRCC documentation standards is key to securing your Canada Study Permit without delays or refusals.

IRCC Financial Standard GIC Verified

Financial Requirements (GIC)

IRCC requires proof of unencumbered living funds and tuition coverage:

  • GIC Certificate: CAD $20,635 deposited in a Canadian bank (Scotiabank, CIBC, ICICI Canada, etc.).
  • 1st Year Tuition Fee: Official payment receipt from your DLI showing 1 full year paid in advance.
  • Sponsor Financials: 3 years ITR, bank statements, and affidavit of support from immediate sponsors.
  • Travel & Settlement: Proof of funds for return flights and initial relocation expenses.

Academic & Permit Criteria

Mandatory documentation needed for Canadian study permit processing:

  • DLI Letter of Acceptance (LOA): Unconditional offer from a designated learning institution.
  • Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL): Verified provincial allocation quota letter.
  • English Language Proficiency: IELTS Academic (min 6.0/6.5) or PTE Academic (min 60).
  • Medical Exam & Police Clearance: Upfront IME from an IRCC panel physician and PCC.
  • Study Plan (SOP): Justification of course choice, financial capability, and ties to home country.

What is a Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) and do I need one?

A Provincial Attestation Letter (PAL) is an official document issued by the province where your DLI is located, confirming that you are counted under the province's federal study permit allocation cap. Under 2026 regulations, most undergraduate and college diploma applicants require a PAL before applying for a Canada Study Permit. Suvira assists in securing your PAL directly through your institution.

How much money is required for a Canada GIC account?

The living expense requirement for Canada Study Permits is CAD $20,635 for a single applicant (in addition to 1 year of tuition fees). You deposit this amount into an approved Canadian financial institution (e.g., Scotiabank, CIBC, ICICI Canada), which disburses monthly allowances to you upon your arrival in Canada.

Can I work while studying in Canada?

Yes. International students holding a valid study permit and studying full-time at a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) are permitted to work up to 24 hours per week off-campus during regular academic semesters, and full-time (up to 40 hours/week) during scheduled breaks and vacations.

Are all programs in Canada eligible for a Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP)?

Most public universities, polytechnics, and qualifying degree/diploma programs at recognized DLIs are PGWP-eligible. Graduates of master's degree programs can qualify for a 3-year PGWP even if the program length is 1 to 2 years. Programs at private colleges under curriculum licensing agreements are generally not PGWP eligible, which is why Suvira only guides you toward verified PGWP-eligible institutions.

What English proficiency tests are accepted for Canada study permits?

Universities and colleges accept IELTS Academic, PTE Academic, and TOEFL iBT. For most diploma programs, an overall IELTS score of 6.0 with no band less than 6.0 is standard. For postgraduate degrees and top universities, an overall IELTS score of 6.5 with no band less than 6.0 is typically required.
